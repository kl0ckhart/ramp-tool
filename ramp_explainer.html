Why does the ramp need to be this long?

The 1‑to‑12 rule, in plain terms. Slide to your door height.

21 inches up

21 feet of ramp
At code slope (1:12), every 1 inch of height needs 1 foot of ramp. So 21 inches up = 21 feet out. It’s that simple — and it can’t be shortened without making the ramp too steep to be safe.

A safe ramp is long and gentle, not steep. That’s the whole reason it takes room.

“But after the first section we’re lower — why keep going?”

True — every section drops you a little. But the height you have left still costs the same: a foot of ramp for every inch. The leftover height doesn’t shrink the ramp — it is the ramp.

What about a landing or a switchback?

Landings are flat — they don’t gain any height. They give a rest or let the ramp turn, but they only add length, never subtract it.

A switchback (folding the ramp back on itself) fits the ramp in a smaller footprint — but the ramp you walk is still 21 feet long. You’re folding the same length into a tighter space, not shortening it.
