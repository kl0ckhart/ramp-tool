How to enter the survey
Type one line for each section of the path:
  A to B: 4 — from A to B, the ground drops 4 inches
  B to C: 12
  C to D: 2
Use as many points as you need (A, B, C, D, E…).

To see where the points are, add a position line for each — feet from the ramp start (0, 0):
  A: 0, 0
  B: 18, 0
  C: 24, 8
Any point with a position is drawn, labeled, on the main plan — and the side view uses those spacings automatically.

Why does the ramp need to be this long?

The 1-to-12 rule. Slide to the top deck height.

Top deck height: in
21 inches up
↓
21 feet of ramp
length of ramp only — not counting rests or landings
At code slope (1:12), every 1 inch of height needs 1 foot of ramp. So 21 in up = 21 ft out — it can’t be shortened without being too steep to be safe.

“After the first section we’re lower — why keep going?”
The height you have left still costs a foot per inch. It doesn’t shrink the ramp — it is the ramp.

Landings & switchbacks: flat landings don’t gain height — they only add length. A switchback folds the same 21 ft into a smaller footprint; you still walk it all.
