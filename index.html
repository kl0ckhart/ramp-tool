How to enter the survey
Shoot the benchmark at the deck base, then the rod reading at points going out along the ramp line. One line each — distance: reading:
  BM: 4' 2-3/8" — benchmark reading at the deck (0 ft)
  13: 3' 1/4" — reading 13 ft out
  21: 2' 5-1/2" — reading 21 ft out

A smaller reading means higher ground, so rises and falls both work with no plus or minus — mixed up-and-down is fine. Distances are feet from the deck; readings take feet-inches (4' 2-3/8") or plain inches (50 3/8). Survey out to about where the ramp foot will land.

Your benchmark can be anywhere — it just sets the reference. Shoot the deck base at 0 (0: reading) so the ramp is anchored there. If the BM happens to sit at the deck base, a single BM: line covers both.

Why does the ramp need to be this long?

The 1-to-12 rule. Slide to the top deck height.

Top deck height: in
21 inches up
↓
21 feet of ramp
length of ramp only — not counting rests or landings
At code slope (1:12), every 1 inch of height needs 1 foot of ramp. So 21 in up = 21 ft out — it can’t be shortened without being too steep to be safe.

“After the first section we’re lower — why keep going?”
The height you have left still costs a foot per inch. It doesn’t shrink the ramp — it is the ramp.

Landings & switchbacks: flat landings don’t gain height — they only add length. A switchback folds the same 21 ft into a smaller footprint; you still walk it all.
